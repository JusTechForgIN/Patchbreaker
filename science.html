Applications of Reaction Rates

Understanding how temperature, concentration, surface area, catalysts, and inhibitors affect chemical reactions in everyday life.

⚛️ What is Collision Theory?

Collision theory is a scientific model that explains how chemical reactions occur and why different reactions happen at different speeds. For a reaction to occur, particles must collide with sufficient energy and in an appropriate orientation. Factors that change the frequency or energy of collisions can therefore affect the rate of a chemical reaction.

🔬 Applications in Daily Life

Five important applications of reaction rates explained through Collision Theory.

APPLICATION 01

🥫 Food Preservation

🌡️ Temperature 🧪 Concentration ⚗️ Inhibitors

Scientific Explanation & Collision Theory

Temperature: Refrigeration and freezing lower the temperature, which decreases the average kinetic energy of molecules. This causes particles to move slower, leading to fewer overall collisions and fewer collisions that meet the required activation energy. As a result, cellular respiration in microorganisms and enzymatic decay processes are drastically slowed.

Concentration: Techniques like vacuum sealing or using nitrogen flush packaging decrease the concentration of oxygen, reducing the frequency of collisions between oxygen molecules and food lipids, thereby preventing oxidation, which causes rancidity.

Salting or sugaring draws water out of food cells via osmosis, lowering the water available to decay-causing bacteria.

Inhibitors: Chemical preservatives such as sodium benzoate and potassium sorbate act as inhibitors that interfere with reaction pathways involved in spoilage, slowing spoilage reactions.

⚛️ Collision Theory Connection:

Lower temperature reduces particle kinetic energy, while reducing oxygen or available water lowers the opportunities for damaging reactions to occur. Preservatives can also interfere with chemical pathways involved in food spoilage.

🏠 Everyday Applications:

Refrigerators, freezers, vacuum-sealed food, salted foods, sugary foods, canned products, and foods containing approved preservatives.

APPLICATION 02

🏭 Materials Production

⚗️ Catalysts 🧪 Concentration 🌡️ Temperature

Scientific Explanation & Collision Theory

Catalysts: Industrial manufacturing, such as synthesizing plastics, fertilizers, and pharmaceuticals, relies heavily on catalysts. Catalysts provide an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. This allows a higher fraction of molecular collisions to become successful without requiring extremely high temperatures.

Concentration: Increasing reactant concentration places more reactant particles in a given volume, increasing collision frequency.

Temperature: Optimizing temperature gives reactant particles sufficient kinetic energy to overcome the activation energy barrier and produce products more efficiently.

⚛️ Collision Theory Connection:

More concentrated reactants collide more often, while increased temperature increases particle kinetic energy. Catalysts lower the activation energy needed for successful collisions.

🏠 Everyday Applications:

Production of plastics, fertilizers, pharmaceuticals, steel, and other manufactured materials.

APPLICATION 03

🔥 Fire Control

🫧 Concentration 🌡️ Temperature 🔥 Surface Area

Scientific Explanation & Collision Theory

Concentration: Fire extinguishers using carbon dioxide or chemical foam displace surrounding air, lowering the concentration of oxygen. Without sufficient oxygen particles available to react with fuel, combustion can stop.

Temperature: Water absorbs thermal energy because of its high specific heat capacity. This lowers the temperature of burning material below its ignition point, reducing the kinetic energy of particles and slowing the combustion reaction.

Surface Area: Fine fuels such as dry leaves, brush, and sawdust have a large surface-area-to-volume ratio. This exposes more material to oxygen and can increase combustion. Removing these materials can limit rapid fire growth.

⚛️ Collision Theory Connection:

Combustion requires fuel and oxygen particles to collide with enough energy. Removing oxygen, lowering temperature, or reducing exposed fuel can decrease successful collisions.

🏠 Everyday Applications:

Fire extinguishers, water used by firefighters, firebreaks, and removal of dry vegetation around wildfire-prone areas.

APPLICATION 04

🌎 Pollution Prevention

⚗️ Catalysts 🧪 Concentration

Scientific Explanation & Collision Theory

Catalysts: Modern automobiles use catalytic converters containing catalysts such as platinum, palladium, and rhodium. These catalysts help convert harmful exhaust substances, including carbon monoxide and unburned hydrocarbons, into less harmful products.

Concentration: Industrial power plants can use wet scrubbers containing basic solutions to react with acidic sulfur dioxide gas. Increasing contact between the neutralizing material and pollutant molecules can promote the desired reactions.

⚛️ Collision Theory Connection:

Catalysts make successful reactions easier by providing a pathway with lower activation energy. Increasing contact or concentration can also increase the frequency of collisions between reactant particles.

🏠 Everyday Applications:

Vehicle catalytic converters and industrial pollution-control systems help reduce harmful substances released into the environment.

APPLICATION 05

🔩 Corrosion

🧪 Concentration 🔥 Surface Area ⚗️ Inhibitors

Scientific Explanation & Collision Theory

Concentration & Protective Barriers: Rusting of iron requires contact with oxygen and moisture. Painting, oiling, or galvanizing iron with zinc creates a protective barrier that reduces direct contact between the metal and its environment.

Surface Area: Smooth, polished metals generally have less exposed surface area than rough or finely divided materials, reducing the area available for reactions.

Corrosion Inhibitors: Coolants and industrial fluid systems can contain corrosion inhibitors. These compounds can form protective films on metal surfaces and slow oxidation-related processes.

⚛️ Collision Theory Connection:

Protective coatings reduce direct contact between reactants and the metal. Reducing exposed surface area decreases the number of locations where reactions can occur, while inhibitors interfere with corrosion processes.

🏠 Everyday Applications:

Painting metal gates, oiling machinery, galvanizing steel, polishing metals, and using corrosion-inhibiting substances in industrial systems.

🧠 Key Takeaway

The rate of a chemical reaction can be influenced by factors such as temperature, concentration, surface area, catalysts, and inhibitors. Collision Theory helps explain these effects by focusing on how often particles collide, how much energy they have, and whether their collisions are successful.
